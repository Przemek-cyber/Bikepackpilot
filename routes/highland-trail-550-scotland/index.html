Highland Trail 550: bikepacking plan & riding days

A 550-mile self-supported mountain bike loop from Tyndrum through the remote Scottish Highlands, mixing singletrack, dirt roads and quiet tarmac, with plenty of hike-a-bike. It's one of the toughest bikepacking routes in the UK.

About the route

  • The Highland Trail is a self-supported mountain bike route of 550 miles (885 km) with over 16,000 m of climbing, starting from Tyndrum.
  • Alan Goldsmith first conceived it as a training route for the Colorado Trail Race, and it grew into one of the toughest self-supported off-road time trials in the world.
  • Most riders in the mass start or time-trial format finish in 4-8 days. The 'summer version' touring route suggests 40-50 miles a day over 11-14 days.
  • The majority of the route is on good trails and quiet roads, but it includes some boggy and very technical sections.

Planning notes

  • Expect hike-a-bike on the most spectacular sections, notably the Fisherfield crossing and the Achnashellach/Torridon area. Weather is changeable, so carry proper wet and cold gear.
  • Tyndrum is reachable by train from Glasgow, which takes a few hours. Bike space is limited, so reserve ahead.
