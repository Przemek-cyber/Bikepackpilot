Silk Road Mountain Race Route (Kyrgyzstan, 2026 edition): bikepacking plan & riding days

A fixed-route, unsupported bikepacking race through Kyrgyzstan's Tian Shan mountains. The 2026 edition runs 2,052 km from Talas to Cholpon-Ata on Lake Issyk-Kul, on gravel, old Soviet roads and some hike-a-bike.

About the route

  • The Silk Road Mountain Race is a fixed-route, unsupported, single-stage cycling race through the mountains of Kyrgyzstan. The clock doesn't stop and there are no prizes.
  • The route changes every year. Riders can usually expect about 1,900 km, 30,000 m of climbing and around 14 days, according to the organisers.
  • The eighth edition (2026) is 2,052 km with roughly 35,362 m of climbing. It starts in Talas in the northwest and finishes in Cholpon-Ata on the north shore of Lake Issyk-Kul.
  • It follows gravel, single and double track and old Soviet roads that have fallen into disrepair, with very little tarmac. There is some hike-a-bike and long gaps between resupply points.

Planning notes

  • Resupply can be very sparse between towns, so carry extra food and plan water stops carefully, especially on remote high sections.
  • Expect hike-a-bike sections and rough, damaged old Soviet roads. Gearing, tyre width and bike choice matter more than speed.
  • The course changes every year, so check the official Mountain Races site for the current GPX and checkpoint details before planning a tour of your own.

FAQ

How many days does it take to bikepack Silk Road Mountain Race?

With BikepackPilot's default pace of 110 km per day on asphalt, the 2,052 km route takes about 25.5 riding days in good conditions and about 43.9 days with moderate headwind and light rain, using the surface mix shown on this page. Rest days are not included.

How long is Silk Road Mountain Race?

about 2,052 km for the 2026 edition (the route changes yearly, typically around 1,900 km), from Talas, Kyrgyzstan to Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan.

What surface is Silk Road Mountain Race?
