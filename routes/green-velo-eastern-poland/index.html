About the route

  • Described by its operator as the longest cycling route in Poland – nearly 2,000 km.
  • Starts in Elbląg and ends in Końskie in the Świętokrzyskie region.
  • Crosses five voivodeships: Warmińsko-Mazurskie (~420 km), Podlaskie (~592 km), Lubelskie (~351 km), Podkarpackie (~428 km) and Świętokrzyskie (~190 km).
  • About 31% of the route runs through protected areas and about 29% through forests.
  • Passes the Biebrza, Białowieża and Narew national parks in Podlasie.

Planning notes

  • Long forest and protected-area sections mean fewer shops: mark your water and food points per 50 km segment before you go.
  • Podkarpackie is the hilliest part – plan shorter days there.
  • Many riders split the route into regional blocks; the plan below shows how many riding days the whole trail takes at a steady pace.

FAQ

How many days does it take to bikepack Green Velo?

With BikepackPilot's default pace of 110 km per day on asphalt, the 1,981 km route takes about 19.2 riding days in good conditions and about 28.3 days with moderate headwind and light rain, using the surface mix shown on this page. Rest days are not included.

How long is Green Velo?

nearly 2,000 km (the official section lengths add up to about 1,981 km), from Elbląg, Poland to Końskie, Poland.

What surface is Green Velo?

For this plan we assume 70% asphalt, 30% gravel (estimated mix for this plan). In BikepackPilot you set the surface for each 50 km segment yourself.
