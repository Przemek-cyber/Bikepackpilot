Baltic Trail – Szlak Rowerowy Wokół Bałtyku (R10 / EuroVelo 10 / Velo Baltica, Poland): bikepacking plan & riding days

The Polish stretch of EuroVelo 10 follows the Baltic coast from Świnoujście on the German border to the Vistula Lagoon and Braniewo. Expect a mix of coastal cycle paths, forest tracks and some sandy sections.

About the route

  • R10 is the common Polish name for the Polish section of EuroVelo 10, the loop around the Baltic Sea; Velo Baltica mainly refers to the part in West Pomerania.
  • The whole EuroVelo 10 loop is about 9,100 km through nine countries, and it has no fixed start point or direction.
  • The Polish section is given as about 588 km including the Hel Peninsula, or about 533 km on the shorter variant without it.
  • In Poland the route runs through West Pomeranian, Pomeranian and Warmian-Masurian voivodeships, passing Świnoujście, Międzyzdroje, Dziwnów, Rewal, Kołobrzeg, Darłowo, Ustka, Łeba, Władysławowo, Gdynia, Sopot, Gdańsk, Elbląg, Frombork and Braniewo.
  • Despite its name, R10 often leaves the immediate seashore, which some riders criticise.

Planning notes

  • Surface is mixed: asphalt, gravel, forest tracks, and some sandy or muddy patches. The Rowy–Łeba stretch is often described as one of the weakest, with earth, sand and worn slabs, so a tyre that can handle gravel helps.
  • Terrain is mostly flat, so daily distances of about 60–100 km are realistic. Many riders cover the Polish coast in roughly 6–8 days.
  • The route swings inland in several places, so carry food and water for stretches away from beach towns. Resort towns are the easiest places to resupply.
